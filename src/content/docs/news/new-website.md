jkit-project.dev used to be three pages. It's now a documentation site with search, built to
answer questions for people at every stage:

- **New to Java?** [Java basics](/start/java-basics/) explains JDKs, vendors, LTS, `JAVA_HOME`
  and build tools, and the [quickstart](/start/quickstart/) gets you from nothing to a working JDK
  in five minutes.
- **Coming from something else?** [Why jkit](/why/overview/) compares jkit with a manual install,
  SDKMAN, mise, asdf and OS packages, honestly, and [Migrating](/guides/migrating/) shows how to
  move over without breaking a team's setup.
- **Setting up a team or CI?** There are guides for [projects](/guides/projects/),
  [teams](/guides/teams/), [CI](/guides/ci/), [Docker](/guides/docker/),
  [IDEs](/guides/ides/) and [corporate networks](/guides/corporate-networks/).
- **Looking something up?** Every command has its own [reference page](/reference/commands/install/),
  along with [project files](/reference/project-files/), [settings](/reference/configuration/),
  [JSON output](/reference/json/) and [exit codes](/reference/exit-codes/).
- **Using an AI agent?** The [AI agents guide](/guides/ai-agents/) has instructions to paste into
  `AGENTS.md` or `CLAUDE.md`, and the whole site is available as
  [llms.txt](/llms/) for language models.

Changes to jkit are listed in [What's new](/whats-new/), and posts like this one live here in
News. If a page is wrong or missing something, every page has an edit link at the bottom.
